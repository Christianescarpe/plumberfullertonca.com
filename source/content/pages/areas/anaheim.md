Anaheim, California is the largest city in Orange County, situated approximately 4.2 straight-line miles south of the Fullerton city center. Bordering Fullerton along the 91 freeway, Lincoln Avenue, and Orangethorpe corridors, Anaheim encompasses vast commercial entertainment districts, post-war residential single-family tract subdivisions, dense multi-family apartment communities, and heavy industrial corridors.

## Anaheim Public Utilities and municipal infrastructure {#anaheim-utilities}

Anaheim operates as an independent municipal entity with its own public utility divisions:

- **Anaheim Public Utilities (Water Division):** Potable water in Anaheim is managed by Anaheim Public Utilities (APU), a city-owned public utility providing water and electrical power. APU draws water from the local Orange County groundwater basin and imported Metropolitan Water District supplies. Fullerton municipal water division numbers do not apply in Anaheim.
- **Sewer Collection Infrastructure:** The City of Anaheim Public Works Department maintains municipal sewer collection mains, with regional wastewater treatment operated by the Orange County Sanitation District (OC San).
- **Natural Gas Utility:** Natural gas service throughout Anaheim is provided by Southern California Gas Company (SoCalGas). Suspected gas leaks should be reported immediately to SoCalGas at (800) 427-2200 or dial 911.

## Slab leak locating in post-war residential neighborhoods {#anaheim-slab-leaks}

West and Central Anaheim feature tens of thousands of post-war single-family homes constructed during the 1950s and 1960s over concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Pressurized copper water supply lines were originally laid directly beneath structural concrete slabs:

Sub-slab copper piping in mid-century Anaheim subdivisions frequently suffers electrolytic corrosion from corrosive soil chemistry alongside internal scouring from turbulent water velocity. When pinhole failures emerge beneath flooring, residents notice localized warm floor tiles, mysterious domestic water bill increases, or damp carpet padding. Independent contractors utilize high-precision electronic acoustic sensors and thermal infrared camera scanning—outlined in our [leak detection guide](/services/leak-detection/#leaks-detected-repaired)—to map underground leak trajectories before executing neat attic bypass reroutes with modern PEX tubing.

## Commercial plumbing and hospitality infrastructure {#anaheim-commercial}

Beyond residential tracts, Anaheim features massive commercial hotel, convention, and restaurant operations surrounding the Anaheim Resort district. Commercial facilities operate under strict Orange County Health Care Agency and municipal plumbing codes, requiring grease interceptor maintenance, high-pressure [hydro-jetting services](/services/drain-cleaning/#hydro-jetting-service), and certified annual [backflow prevention assembly testing](/services/commercial-plumbing/#commercial-backflow).

Q: Do Fullerton municipal water emergency numbers apply to Anaheim?
A: No. Anaheim is an independent charter city with its own municipal utility, Anaheim Public Utilities (APU). City of Fullerton emergency numbers, utility policies, and permit fee schedules apply exclusively to properties located within Fullerton city limits.

Q: How can a slab leak in an Anaheim home be repaired without damaging tile flooring?
A: Licensed plumbers solve Anaheim slab leaks non-destructively through an overhead bypass reroute. The failed underground copper run is isolated and capped at both ends, and a durable PEX-a water line is routed cleanly through attic spaces and dropped down interior wall cavities to feed the fixture.

Q: Are permits required for water heater replacements in Anaheim?
A: Yes. The City of Anaheim Building & Safety division requires a plumbing permit and final inspection for all residential water heater replacements (both conventional storage and tankless) to ensure compliance with California seismic strapping and venting safety codes.
