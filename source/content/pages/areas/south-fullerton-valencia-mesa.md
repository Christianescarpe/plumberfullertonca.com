## Post-war residential slab foundations and copper pipe failures {#south-slab-leaks}

Single-family tract homes constructed across South Fullerton and the Valencia Mesa area during the 1950s and 1960s were built primarily on concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Potable water lines were installed as soft copper coils routed through the dirt or sand sub-base beneath the structural concrete:

Over 50 to 65 years of continuous exposure to subterranean moisture, alkaline soil chemistry, and pressurized water velocity friction, copper lines develop microscopic pinhole breaches known as "slab leaks." Homeowners often notice damp baseboards, unexplained warm spots on flooring, the sound of running water behind walls, or sharp increases in their City of Fullerton water bills. 

Licensed plumbing contractors deploy non-invasive electronic acoustic microphones and infrared cameras—detailed in our [leak detection guide](/services/leak-detection/#leaks-detected-repaired)—to pinpoint leaks, frequently recommending overhead PEX repiping reroutes through attic cavities to eliminate underground pipes permanently without breaking finished flooring.

## Commercial facilities, warehouses, and industrial plumbing {#industrial-corridors}

The southern perimeter of Fullerton along Orangethorpe Avenue and the rail corridor is home to extensive manufacturing facilities, distribution centers, commercial automotive shops, and food processing plants:

- **Heavy-Duty Drainage and Floor Sinks:** Commercial facilities require specialized drainage solutions, including industrial trench drains, chemical-resistant piping, and heavy-duty solids interceptors.
- **Backflow Prevention & Cross-Connection Control:** Industrial facilities utilizing chemical injection, commercial boilers, or cooling towers must install certified [backflow prevention assemblies](/services/specialty-plumbing/#backflow-preventers) at the municipal water meter to protect the city's drinking water distribution system, subject to mandatory annual testing overseen by the City of Fullerton Water Division.
- **High-Capacity Commercial Water Heaters:** Restaurants and multi-tenant commercial centers require high-recovery commercial gas boilers or multi-unit tankless arrays to meet peak sanitation demands.

## Water heater replacements and seismic strapping standards {#south-water-heaters}

Aging water heaters in South Fullerton residential properties require code-compliant replacement adhering to California Plumbing Code and City of Fullerton municipal standards. All installations require dual heavy-gauge seismic earthquake strapping anchored into structural wall studs, a code-approved thermal expansion tank on the cold water inlet line, and a full-bore temperature-pressure relief valve discharge line plumbed to an approved exterior drainage location, as detailed in our [water heater guide](/services/water-heaters/#water-heater-install-or-replace).

:::faq
Q: What causes slab leaks in South Fullerton mid-century homes?
A: Slab leaks occur when copper water pipes buried directly under concrete slab foundations corrode externally from contact with rocky backfill and acidic soils, or internally from water velocity turbulence. Over decades, this creates pinholes that allow pressurized water to escape under the foundation.

Q: Is it necessary to break my concrete floor to fix a slab leak?
A: No. In most cases, contractors recommend an overhead pipe reroute, capping the leaking underground line and installing a brand new, continuous PEX or copper supply pipe through your attic or interior walls. This preserves your flooring and eliminates future leaks along that line.

Q: Are commercial plumbing permits required in South Fullerton?
A: Yes. All commercial plumbing modifications—including grease trap installations, backflow device additions, restroom tenant improvements, and commercial water heater replacements—require plan review and building permits issued by the City of Fullerton Building & Safety division.
