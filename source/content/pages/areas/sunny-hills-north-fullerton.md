Sunny Hills and North Fullerton (ZIP code 92835) encompasses some of North Orange County's most desirable custom estate residences, scenic hillside topography, and mid-century modern architectural developments. Bordered by Laguna Lake Park and the Coyote Hills, properties in this district feature expansive square footage, multiple luxury bathrooms, extensive landscape irrigation, custom swimming pools, and complex domestic water heating configurations.

Due to significant elevation changes in North Fullerton, municipal incoming water pressure can vary dramatically between hilltop ridges and canyon basins. Maintaining calibrated pressure-reducing valves (PRVs) is essential to safeguard luxury fixtures and prevent slab pinhole leaks.

## Topography, elevation changes, and water pressure management {#pressure-zones}

The rolling terrain of Sunny Hills requires the City of Fullerton Water Division to operate specialized water distribution pressure zones. To deliver adequate water volume to custom homes perched atop scenic ridges along Bastanchury Road and Rosecrans Avenue, static water pressure in the municipal distribution mains in lower street elevations frequently exceeds 100 to 110 PSI:

Unregulated high municipal pressure exerts destructive hydraulic stress on indoor residential plumbing systems. Pressure exceeding 80 PSI voids manufacturer warranties on washing machines, dishwashers, and water filtration equipment, causes loud "water hammer" pipe shuddering, and accelerates pinhole corrosion in copper supply lines. Under California Plumbing Code Section 608.2, every residence subjected to municipal pressure above 80 PSI must maintain an approved, calibrated Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) on the main incoming water riser. Technicians test static pressure and replace aging PRVs to keep household pressure within the safe 50 to 70 PSI operating range, as detailed in our [pipe repair guide](/services/pipe-water-line-repair/#water-pressure-problems).

## Mid-century concrete slab foundations and hidden leaks {#slab-leaks}

Custom single-family residences constructed across Sunny Hills during the 1960s and 1970s predominantly feature uninsulated concrete slab-on-grade foundations with pressurized copper water lines routed directly through subterranean sand and gravel fill. 

Over decades of service, thermal expansion cycles and soil friction create microscopic pinhole leaks along underground copper pipe walls. Because water migrates under the concrete slab before surfacing, property owners often detect warm floor tiles, mysterious wet carpet patches, or damp baseboards far from the actual pipe rupture. Licensed contractors utilize non-invasive electronic acoustic leak detectors and thermal infrared imaging—detailed in our [leak detection guide](/services/leak-detection/#leaks-detected-repaired)—to pinpoint the leak location without breaking floor tiles, frequently executing overhead PEX pipe reroutes to resolve the issue permanently.

## High-capacity tankless water heaters and pool plumbing {#estate-plumbing}

Expansive Sunny Hills residences featuring four to six bathrooms often demand high-capacity hot water delivery systems. Converting from dual 50-gallon storage water heaters to high-efficiency [tankless water heating systems](/services/water-heaters/#tankless-water-heater-install) requires evaluating incoming natural gas line sizing. Tankless units demand up to 199,000 BTUs/hr, often requiring a dedicated 3/4-inch or 1-inch gas line from the meter to maintain continuous hot water delivery across multiple simultaneous showers.

Additionally, extensive residential swimming pools, spas, and outdoor entertainment kitchens across North Fullerton require dedicated gas lines, code-compliant backwash drainage air gaps, and certified [backflow prevention assemblies](/services/specialty-plumbing/#backflow-preventers) on automated pool autofill lines.

:::faq
Q: Why is water pressure so high in parts of Sunny Hills?
A: Because of North Fullerton's rolling hills and steep elevation changes, the city's water utility must maintain high pressure in lower elevation mains to pump water effectively to homes on hilltops. If your home sits at a lower elevation, a functional Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) is necessary to keep incoming pressure at safe household levels (below 80 PSI).

Q: How can I tell if my Sunny Hills home has a slab leak?
A: Common indicators include warm spots on concrete or tile flooring, the sound of water running inside walls when no fixtures are active, sudden unexplained jumps in your municipal water bill, or a constantly spinning leak indicator dial on your water meter at the street curb.

Q: Can a tankless water heater handle a large estate home with multiple bathrooms?
A: Yes. For large estate properties with multiple simultaneous hot water demands, plumbers install multi-unit tankless systems connected in parallel with intelligent cascading controllers. These systems modulate burner output dynamically to deliver continuous hot water to multiple showers without pressure or temperature drops.
