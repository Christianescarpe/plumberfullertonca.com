## Leaks Detected & Repaired {#leaks-detected-repaired}

Under-slab foundation leaks—commonly known as "slab leaks"—represent a frequent plumbing failure across mid-century single-family homes and condominiums throughout Fullerton. When copper supply piping was installed beneath concrete slabs during construction in the 1960s through 1980s, pipes were often laid in direct contact with rocky backfill or acidic soils without protective sleeving. Over decades, thermal expansion, water turbulence, and external soil corrosion create pinhole leaks in pressurized hot or cold water lines.

Common symptoms of an active under-slab or hidden wall leak include:

- Unexplained spikes in your City of Fullerton municipal water utility bill despite normal household consumption.
- The sound of rushing or hissing water behind bathroom walls or beneath floors when all indoor fixtures are turned off.
- Noticeably warm or hot spots on concrete slab floors, linoleum, engineered hardwood, or tile.
- The flow indicator dial or digital leak detection triangle on your city water meter continuously spinning when all household taps are shut.
- Damp baseboards, cracked floor tiles, warped floorboards, or sudden moisture along the foundation perimeter.

Licensed plumbing contractors utilize advanced non-destructive diagnostic tools:

- **Electronic Acoustic Microphones:** Highly sensitive ground microphones and acoustic amplifiers detect the ultrasonic vibrations and sound frequencies generated by pressurized water escaping through pinholes in copper pipes beneath concrete slabs.
- **Thermal Infrared Imaging:** Infrared thermography cameras detect temperature variations on flooring surfaces, visually tracing the thermal plume created by leaking subterranean hot water lines.
- **Gas Tracer and Pressure Testing:** Technicians isolate plumbing manifolds, drain residual water, and pressurize the system with non-toxic helium or nitrogen gas. Sniffer probes pinpoint the exact location where tracer gas escapes through flooring cracks.

Once an under-slab leak is located, contractors present targeted repair options: targeted direct slab penetration (jackhammering a small opening through concrete to splice the damaged pipe section) or overhead pipe rerouting (capping the leaking underground line and running a new, seamless PEX or copper supply line through attic or drywall spaces, preserving your concrete flooring intact).

## Comprehensive diagnostic plumbing system inspections {#plumbing-inspection}

A professional Plumbing Inspection evaluates all pressurized potable water supply lines, drainage stacks, fixture valves, and water heating systems throughout your property. Whether you are purchasing an older home in [Downtown Fullerton](/areas/downtown-central-fullerton/), managing multi-family apartment units near [CSUF](/areas/east-fullerton-csuf/), or troubleshooting systemic water pressure issues, a comprehensive inspection provides an objective baseline of your plumbing infrastructure:

- **Static Pressure Testing:** Measuring municipal incoming water pressure at outdoor hose bibbs to verify that pressure-reducing valves (PRVs) maintain household pressure within the safe California Plumbing Code range of 45 to 75 PSI. Excessive pressure (exceeding 80 PSI) stresses fixture valves, bursts flexible appliance hoses, and accelerates pipe corrosion.
- **Supply Valve and Fixture Audits:** Inspecting angle stop shutoff valves under every sink, toilet fill valves, washing machine supply lines, and outdoor spigots for mineral weeping or mechanical binding.
- **Water Heater Safety Verification:** Checking temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valves, seismic earthquake strapping, combustion air draft hoods, and expansion tanks on domestic water heaters.
- **Drainage and Venting Review:** Inspecting accessible crawlspace or attic drainage pipes for hairline fractures, proper slope, and code-compliant fixture venting.

Following the inspection, the attending contractor provides a written diagnostic report outlining identified hazards, recommended repairs, and transparent cost estimates.

:::faq
Q: How does a plumber locate a slab leak without breaking my concrete floor?
A: Professional leak detection specialists use non-invasive acoustic listening discs, ultrasonic listening devices, thermal infrared imaging cameras, and digital line locators. These advanced instruments trace the sound and heat signatures of pressurized water escaping beneath concrete, pinpointing the leak location prior to any excavation.

Q: Is it better to repair a slab leak under the floor or reroute the pipe overhead?
A: In most Southern California slab homes, an overhead pipe reroute—capping the leaking underground pipe and installing a new PEX line through the attic or walls—is the preferred, more durable solution. Rerouting avoids breaking expensive flooring, prevents foundation disturbance, and eliminates future leaks along that underground copper run.

Q: How can I tell if I have a hidden water leak on my property?
A: Turn off all water-using appliances, faucets, and irrigation systems inside and outside your home. Then check your municipal water meter at the street curb. If the small low-flow indicator dial (often a red or blue triangle) continues to spin, pressurized water is escaping somewhere on your property piping.

Q: Does homeowner insurance cover the cost of slab leak detection and repair?
A: Most standard California homeowner insurance policies cover the "tear out" access labor to reach the leak and subsequent repairs for water damage to flooring and drywall, but frequently exclude the cost of the actual plumbing pipe repair itself. Request written diagnostic documentation from your contractor for insurance claims.
