Plumbing fixtures—including kitchen sinks, bathroom lavatories, toilets, showers, bathtubs, and garbage disposals—represent the primary interactive touchpoints of your home's potable water and drainage systems. Whether you are addressing dripping faucets, upgrading to modern water-conserving toilets, replacing a jammed garbage disposal, or undertaking an extensive master bathroom or kitchen remodel in Fullerton, professional installation ensures long-term leak-free reliability and compliance with California standards.

## General plumbing repairs and diagnostic evaluations {#general-plumbing-repair}

General Plumbing Repair encompasses daily maintenance and troubleshooting across residential and commercial water and drainage systems. Running toilets, dripping faucet spouts, vibrating pipes, leaking drain traps under kitchen sinks, and corroded supply shutoffs are routine issues that waste significant potable water and create moisture hazards inside walls and vanities.

Experienced licensed plumbers evaluate the underlying cause of fixture failure—such as excessive municipal incoming water pressure, mineral scale encrustation from the local groundwater supply, or degraded rubber seals—and provide transparent, written repair options.

## Repairing and replacing faucets, valves, and water supply pipes {#faucets-fixtures-and-pipes}

Faucets, Fixtures and Pipes - Repair or Replace addresses worn components that compromise potable water control. Modern ceramic disc cartridges inside single-handle kitchen and bathroom faucets provide smooth operation, but dissolved calcium minerals in Fullerton tap water can score internal silicone seals over time, causing stubborn drips. 

Technicians rebuild or replace leaking faucet cartridges, install new quarter-turn ball valve angle stops beneath sinks, replace corroded braided stainless steel supply hoses, and repair copper or PEX risers inside wall cavities. When plumbing supply lines suffer from extensive corrosion or systemic pressure drops, review our [pipe and water line repair guide](/services/pipe-water-line-repair/#pipe-replacements-repairs) for whole-home repiping solutions.

## Sinks, toilets, and faucet installations {#sinks-toilets-faucets}

Sinks, Toilets & Faucets handle the highest daily volume of household use:

- **Toilet Repairs and Upgrades:** Constant toilet running, weak flushing, phantom cycling, and water weeping around the base are common toilet malfunctions. Technicians replace worn flappers, fill valves, and degraded wax ring floor gaskets. When replacing older 3.5-gallon toilets, contractors install modern WaterSense-certified high-efficiency toilets (HET) consuming 1.28 gallons per flush (GPF) or dual-flush models that comply strictly with California Green Building Standards (CALGreen).
- **Kitchen and Lavatory Sinks:** Installing new undermount stainless steel sinks, cast-iron porcelain basins, or quartz composite sinks requires precise drain waste-and-vent (DWV) rough-in alignment, durable basket strainers, and watertight silicone sealing.

## Installing and replacing garbage disposals {#garbage-disposals}

Garbage Disposals grind soft food waste into fine particles that flow safely through residential drainage lines. When a disposal motor hums without spinning, rattles loudly, or leaks dirty water from the bottom motor housing, professional service is required. 

Technicians clear foreign object jams, reset internal overload protectors, or install new 1/2-HP to 1-HP continuous-feed disposal units equipped with sound insulation and corrosion-resistant stainless steel grinding chambers. If water leaks from the bottom of the disposal casing, the internal motor shaft seal has ruptured, necessitating complete disposal replacement.

California Plumbing Code Section 408.3 mandates that all shower and tub-shower combinations be equipped with pressure-balancing or thermostatic mixing valves. These safety valves automatically compensate for sudden hot or cold water pressure fluctuations (such as a toilet flushing elsewhere in the home), preventing scalding water shocks.

## Bathtub Install or Replace {#bathtub-install-or-replace}

Bathtub Install or Replace projects range from setting freestanding acrylic soaking tubs to replacing cast-iron alcove bathtubs. Installing a bathtub requires leveling the subfloor, securing the waste and overflow kit, soldering brass drain assemblies, and staging rough-in plumbing before wall tile finishes are installed. Plumbers ensure proper trap clearances and watertight gasket seals.

## Shower Install or Replace {#shower-install-or-replace}

Shower Install or Replace involves roughing in multi-function shower valves, diverters, handheld wands, and showerheads, alongside installing code-compliant shower pans and drains. Technicians install shower pan liners, perform required overnight standing water flood tests mandated by City of Fullerton inspectors, and connect drain assemblies to prevent moisture leaks into floor framing.

## New plumbing fixtures installed for renovations {#fixtures-installed}

Fixtures Installed during kitchen and bathroom updates must comply with California Energy Commission Title 20 appliance efficiency standards. In California, residential lavatory faucets must not exceed 1.2 gallons per minute (GPM) at 60 PSI, kitchen faucets are limited to 1.8 GPM, and showerheads must not exceed 1.8 GPM. Licensed contractors ensure that customer-selected designer fixtures comply with California water conservation codes and match existing plumbing rough-in dimensions.

## Rough-in plumbing for home remodels and room additions {#plumbing-for-remodel-or-addition}

:::conditional
Plumbing modifications involving relocated drain stacks, new bathroom additions, or framing alterations require architectural plans, municipal building permits, and formal inspections conducted by the City of Fullerton Building & Safety division.
:::

Plumbing for a Remodel or Addition - Install encompasses the complex rough-in infrastructure required when expanding living spaces, building an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU), or completely reconfiguring a master bathroom or gourmet kitchen:

- **Drain Waste-and-Vent (DWV) Rough-Ins:** Sizing and sloping ABS or cast-iron drain lines to ensure proper gravity flow (minimum 1/4-inch fall per horizontal foot), and extending vent stacks through the roof to prevent trap siphonage and sewer gas intrusion.
- **Water Supply Rough-Ins:** Installing dedicated hot and cold PEX or copper manifolds, balancing water supply fixture units (WSFUs), and roughing in in-wall shower valves.
- **Permits and Inspections:** Licensed C-36 contractors submit plumbing plans, pull building permits, and coordinate required on-site rough-in, water test, and final inspections with City of Fullerton building inspectors.

:::faq
Q: Do I need a municipal building permit to replace a toilet or faucet in Fullerton?
A: No. Replacing existing plumbing fixtures in-kind—such as installing a new toilet in the same location, swapping an old faucet, or replacing a garbage disposal—does not require a city permit. However, if you relocate drain lines, add new fixture rough-ins, or alter water supply piping within walls, a City of Fullerton plumbing permit is required.

Q: What is the difference between a rough-in plumber and a finish plumber?
A: Rough-in plumbing takes place while wall framing is exposed and involves running concealed drain, waste, vent, and water supply pipes according to architectural plans. Finish plumbing occurs after drywall, tile, and cabinetry are completed, involving the final mounting, hookup, and caulking of sinks, faucets, toilets, and trim kits.

Q: Why does my shower water turn scalding hot when someone flushes a toilet?
A: In older homes without modern pressure-balancing shower valves, flushing a toilet draws cold water into the fill tank, causing an immediate drop in cold water pressure at the showerhead while hot water pressure remains constant. Installing a code-compliant pressure-balancing or thermostatic shower valve permanently resolves this issue.

Q: Are low-flow toilets and faucets mandatory in California?
A: Yes. Under California Green Building Standards (CALGreen) and Title 20 regulations, all newly installed residential toilets must not exceed 1.28 gallons per flush (GPF), bathroom lavatory faucets are restricted to a maximum flow rate of 1.2 gallons per minute (GPM), and showerheads must not exceed 1.8 GPM.
:::
